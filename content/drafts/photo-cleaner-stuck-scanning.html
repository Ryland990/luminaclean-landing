Photo cleaner stuck scanning? Fixes for large libraries

You opened a photo cleaner, tapped scan, and the progress bar has barely moved in twenty minutes. On a large iPhone library this is common, and in most cases the app is not frozen. It is waiting on something specific, and once you know what, the fix is usually straightforward.

This is a diagnostic checklist. Work through it in order. The first item resolves the large majority of stuck scans, and the later items cover the rest, ending with how to tell when the problem is the app itself rather than your phone.

Short answer

Most of the time a photo cleaner app stuck scanning is waiting on iCloud. With Optimize iPhone Storage on, your full-resolution originals live in the cloud, and the app has to download each one before it can analyze it. On a 30,000-photo library that download is the scan. Plug in, stay on Wi-Fi, keep the app open, and let it finish. If it still stalls, the fixes below cover indexing, storage headroom, backgrounding, heat, and scanning in smaller slices.

Start with iCloud Photos and Optimize iPhone Storage

This is the single most common cause. If you use iCloud Photos with Optimize iPhone Storage turned on, iOS keeps space-saving versions on the device and stores your full-resolution originals in iCloud. A cleaner needs the original to check a photo for duplicates, similar shots, or blur, so for every photo that is not already on the phone, it has to download the file first.

On a library of 30,000 photos that download is the scan. The analysis itself is fast; pulling tens of thousands of originals over Wi-Fi is what takes the time, and on a weak or intermittent connection a scan can look frozen when it is queued behind a slow transfer.

To move it along: plug the phone in, connect to a stable Wi-Fi network, keep the cleaner open in the foreground, and let it run. If you want the files local before you start, open Settings > Photos and switch to Download and Keep Originals, then wait for the download to complete before scanning. One caution here, since iCloud ties your phone and the cloud together: understand how deletions sync before you clean, which we cover in what happens when a cleaner app deletes photos from iCloud.

iOS is still indexing after an update or restore

After an iOS update, a restore from backup, or setting up a new phone, the Photos app re-runs Apple's own on-device analysis. This is the work behind People, scene recognition, and Memories, and Apple schedules it to run while the phone is locked and charging, often overnight. During that window a third-party scan competes with Apple's indexing for the same processor and photo access, so both run slower.

If you updated or restored in the last day or two, leave the phone on a charger overnight, locked, on Wi-Fi. Give the built-in indexing time to finish, then retry the cleaner's scan the next day. You will often find it completes in a fraction of the time.

Low free storage makes the scan thrash

A scan needs working headroom. As it runs, it writes temporary data and caches the images it is analyzing, and if the phone is nearly full there is nowhere to put that work. iOS starts shuffling data to free space, and everything slows down, including the scan you are waiting on.

Free up a few gigabytes before you scan. Clearing some space first gives the cleaner room to work and often unblocks a scan that kept stalling near the same point. Backgrounded apps and thermal throttling

iOS suspends heavy work when an app leaves the foreground. If you switch to another app, answer a message, or let the screen lock, the system can pause the scan to save power. When you come back, it resumes, but the total time stretches and it can look stuck.

For a big scan, keep the cleaner on screen and stop the display from sleeping. Open Settings > Display & Brightness > Auto-Lock and set it to Never while the scan runs, then change it back afterward. Plugging in helps here too, because a long scan drains the battery quickly.

Heat is the other throttle. Analyzing thousands of images keeps the processor busy, and a hot phone deliberately slows itself to cool down, which drags out the scan. If the device feels warm, take the case off, move it off a charging pad or out of direct sun, and give it a minute. A cooler phone finishes faster.

Restart, then scan in slices

If the checklist so far has not helped, restart the phone and try once more. A plain restart clears whatever background state was interfering and is worth doing before you conclude anything is wrong. Retry the scan on a charger, on Wi-Fi, with the screen kept on.

When a full-library scan keeps stalling, stop trying to do it all at once and break it into slices instead. Two ways to slice work well:

  • By year. Scan one year at a time so the app only has to pull and analyze a few thousand photos per pass. LuminaClean's Flashback runs year-scoped scans for exactly this.
  • By media type. Filter to one kind of item, such as screenshots or large videos, and clear that group first. LuminaClean lets you filter by file size and media type so you can carve the library into smaller jobs.

Slicing turns one enormous scan that may never complete into a series of short ones that do, and you start recovering space after the first pass instead of waiting for the whole library.

When it's the app, not your phone

If you have worked through everything above and a scan still will not finish, the limitation may be in the app's design rather than your device. The clearest tell is how the app handles progress. Some cleaners re-scan the entire library from zero every time you open them and never persist where they got to. On a small library you never notice. On a very large one, a scan that restarts from the beginning after every interruption can loop without ever reaching the end.

Cleaners built for large libraries take a different approach: they process in batches, keep their place, and let you scope each scan so no single pass has to hold the whole library at once. On-device processing also removes the iCloud download bottleneck once your originals are local, because the analysis never leaves the phone.

At some point the honest conclusion is that a given app cannot handle the size of your library, and the fix is to switch rather than keep retrying. Look for an app that runs on-device, processes in batches, and gives you year and media-type filters so you can work through a large library in manageable pieces.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my photo cleaner app get stuck scanning?

The most common reason is iCloud Photos with Optimize iPhone Storage turned on. Your full-resolution originals live in iCloud, and the cleaner has to download each one before it can analyze it. On a library of tens of thousands of photos, that download is the slow part, and over a weak connection the scan can look frozen when it is waiting on files rather than stuck. Other causes include iOS still indexing after an update, low free storage, the app being backgrounded, or the phone overheating.

Does iCloud Optimize iPhone Storage slow down scans?

Yes. With Optimize iPhone Storage on, iOS keeps smaller versions on the device and stores the originals in iCloud. A cleaner needs the full original to check for duplicates, similar shots, or blur, so it triggers a download for anything not already on the phone. To speed things up, plug in, stay on Wi-Fi, keep the app in the foreground, and let it run, or temporarily switch to Download and Keep Originals in Settings so the files are local before you scan.

Should I keep the screen on while a photo cleaner scans?

For a large scan, yes. iOS suspends heavy background work when an app leaves the foreground, so switching apps or letting the screen lock can pause the scan. Keep the cleaner open and on screen, and set Auto-Lock to Never in Settings > Display & Brightness for the duration of a big scan. Plugging in helps too, since a long scan drains the battery and generates heat that can throttle the processor.

What can I do if the app still can't finish scanning my library?

Scan in smaller slices instead of the whole library at once. Some apps let you scan by year or filter by media type, so you can process a few thousand photos at a time and let progress build up. If the app restarts from zero every launch and never keeps its place, a very large library may never finish in one sitting, and that is a sign to switch to a cleaner that processes in batches and lets you scope each scan.
