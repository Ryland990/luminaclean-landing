Is CleanMyPhone worth it? Honest review and alternatives (2026)

Short answer

CleanMyPhone is a well-built iPhone photo cleaner from MacPaw, a developer with a long track record. Its design is polished and its duplicate and similar-photo detection is capable. The catch is the model: it is subscription-only at $7.99/month or $36.99/year, with a limited free experience. If you clean your library a few times a year, that recurring fee is the main reason to weigh a one-time-purchase app or Apple’s free built-in Duplicates album instead.

CleanMyPhone comes up often when people search for a way to clear photo clutter on an iPhone, and for good reason. It is made by MacPaw, the company behind CleanMyMac, and carries a 4.6-star rating across roughly 23,000 ratings on the US App Store. That is a real reputation, not a fly-by-night cleaner.

This review looks at what the app does well, where reviewers push back, what it costs, and how it compares with two alternatives: LuminaClean (our own app, disclosed up front) and the Duplicates tool Apple builds into Photos. The aim is to help you decide whether CleanMyPhone fits you or whether something cheaper or free does the same job.

What CleanMyPhone is

CleanMyPhone is an iOS app that scans your photo and video library and helps you remove clutter. It grew out of Gemini Photos, an earlier MacPaw gallery cleaner, and it now splits its work into a few modules: a Declutter view for removing junk shots, an Organize view that sorts photos into AI categories, and a network speed test. It requires iOS 17 or later and runs its analysis on-device, so your photos are not uploaded to a server.

MacPaw is an established software company with more than a decade in the Mac cleaning space, and that background shows. The interface is clean, the animations are smooth, and the review flow is easy to follow. If you have used CleanMyMac on a desktop, the family resemblance is obvious.

What CleanMyPhone does well

Credit where it is due. Several things about CleanMyPhone hold up under scrutiny:

  • Polished design. The app looks and feels professional. Scanning, sorting, and swiping through results are smooth, and the visual grouping of photos is easy to parse.
  • Capable duplicate and similar detection. It finds exact duplicates and visually similar shots (near-duplicates from bursts or multiple attempts at the same scene), which is the part of photo cleanup that Apple’s own tools miss.
  • An established developer behind it. MacPaw has been shipping cleaning software for years and is not going to vanish next quarter. Support, updates, and a public company reputation all count for something.
  • On-device processing. Photos stay on your phone during analysis. In a category where some apps quietly upload images, that matters.
  • You stay in control of deletions. The app groups candidates and lets you decide what to remove rather than wiping things automatically.

If you value a refined experience from a name-brand developer, CleanMyPhone delivers it.

Common complaints in reviews

Set against the genuine strengths, three criticisms come up repeatedly in reviews and forum threads. None of them make CleanMyPhone a bad app, but they are the reasons some people look elsewhere.

The free version is very limited

Beyond the 3-day trial, most cleaning is locked behind the subscription. A common complaint in reviews is that you cannot get meaningful, ongoing use without paying, so the free download functions more as a preview than a usable free tier.

The scope is mostly photos

The name suggests broad phone cleanup, but the app centers on the photo and video library plus contact deduplication and a speed test. A common complaint in reviews is that iOS does not let any third-party app clear system caches or junk files the way a desktop cleaner can, so the “phone” framing can feel larger than what the app does.

The price feels high for a periodic task

Photo cleanup is something most people do occasionally, not daily. A common complaint in reviews is that a renewing subscription feels steep for a chore you might run three or four times a year, especially when a one-time purchase or a free built-in tool can cover the same need.

Who each option suits

There is no single winner here. The right pick depends on how you clean and how you feel about subscriptions.

CleanMyPhone suits you if

You want a polished app from a well-known developer, you clean often enough that a subscription earns its keep, and the yearly fee does not bother you. The design and the MacPaw name are the draw, and both are legitimate.

A one-time-purchase app suits you if

You want the same similar-photo detection but clean only a few times a year and would rather not renew a subscription for it. A lifetime purchase like LuminaClean’s $17.99 option, or a free tier you can return to, fits that pattern better. Apple’s built-in tools suit you if

Your clutter is mostly exact duplicates and you do not want to install anything. The free Duplicates album handles identical copies well. It will not find near-duplicates or blurry shots, but if that is all you need, you do not need to pay anyone.

The bottom line: CleanMyPhone is a good app held back, for many people, by its pricing model. The design is strong, the detection works, and MacPaw is a developer you can trust with your photo library. If the recurring fee is the issue, a one-time-purchase cleaner gives you the same similar-photo detection without the renewal, and Apple’s free Duplicates album covers exact copies at no cost. Match the tool to how often you clean, and the “worth it” question mostly answers itself.
