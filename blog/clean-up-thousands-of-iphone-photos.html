How do I clean up thousands of iPhone photos without checking every one?

How do I clean up tens of thousands of iPhone photos without checking every single one?

Work by pile, not by photo: merge Apple’s Duplicates album first (one Select All covers every set), then clear screenshots, big videos, bursts and saved chat images, and only then go year by year through what’s left. Everything you delete waits in Recently Deleted for 30 days, so you can move fast and still undo a mistake.

Short answer

Do it in this order, because each step clears a lot with very little judgment. 1. Duplicates: Photos > Collections > Utilities > Duplicates > Select > Select All > Merge. 2. Screenshots: Collections > Media Types > Screenshots, tap Select and slide your finger across rows. 3. Big videos: filter the library to Videos and start with the longest clips. 4. Bursts and chat images. 5. Everything else, one year at a time, or not at all. 6. Check Recently Deleted before you empty it.

The steps below use iOS 26 names: the old Albums tab is now Collections, and Duplicates and Recently Deleted sit under Utilities. Every menu path is taken from Apple’s own support pages. If you want the basics of selecting and deleting in bulk, our guide to deleting multiple photos on iPhone covers them. This page is about the order to work in when the library is too big to look at.

Why photo-by-photo review stalls on a big library

At two seconds a photo, 30,000 photos is more than 16 hours of decisions. Most people stop after the first evening, a few hundred photos lighter and with no visible change in storage.

The fix is to stop deciding about single photos. A big library is a handful of piles: exact copies, screenshots, long videos, burst frames, images saved from group chats, and the real photos you took. The first five piles are mostly junk and can go in bulk. The last pile is where your memories are, and it’s the only one that needs care.

One rule before you start: if you use iCloud Photos, Apple says a photo you delete on one device is deleted on every device signed in to the same Apple Account. Back up anything irreplaceable somewhere else first.

Step 1: Merge every exact duplicate in one go

How to tell it applies: you’ve imported photos twice, restored from more than one backup, or saved the same image from several chats. If the Duplicates collection exists at all, you have some.

What to do: open Photos, tap Collections, scroll down and tap Duplicates under Utilities. Don’t merge set by set. Tap Select, then Select All, then Merge, and confirm with Merge [number] Items. Apple says merging keeps one photo with the highest image quality and the relevant data from the set, and moves the other copies to Recently Deleted.

This is the only step where iOS makes the choice for you, and it only picks between copies of the same photo. If Duplicates isn’t there, the phone hasn’t found any yet; our guide on deleting duplicate photos on iPhone explains why, and what Apple’s album leaves out.

Step 2: Clear screenshots by the row

How to tell it applies: almost everyone. Screenshots pile up from maps, tickets, recipes, memes and receipts, and most were useful for a day.

What to do: open Photos, tap Collections, scroll down to Media Types, then tap Screenshots. Tap Select, then slide your finger across a row and keep dragging down the screen to pick up whole rows at once. Apple describes this as sliding your finger across multiple photos to select more than one. Then tap the Trash button and confirm.

Start at the oldest end. A screenshot from three years ago is almost never needed, so you can sweep those years without reading them. Slow down only for the last few months, where boarding passes and order numbers live. Our guide to cleaning up iPhone screenshots has more on what to keep.

Not ready to delete them? Hide them from the main library instead. Tap the menu button in the library, tap View Options, then tap Screenshots. They stay in Media Types and stop cluttering the grid while you deal with everything else.

Step 3: Go after the big videos

How to tell it applies: Settings > General > iPhone Storage shows Photos as the biggest category, but deleting photos barely moves the number. Video is usually where the space is.

What to do: in the Photos library, tap the menu button, tap Filter, then tap Videos. Each thumbnail shows its length, so start with the long ones: pocket recordings, full concerts, screen recordings you never watched. A single long 4K clip can take more space than hundreds of photos. Tap All Items when you’re done to clear the filter.

Photos doesn’t sort by file size. Our guide to finding large videos on iPhone covers other ways to see which clips are biggest.

Step 4: Bursts and images saved from chats

Bursts: one press of the shutter can leave dozens of frames. Tap the Burst thumbnail, then tap Burst (number). Apple marks the suggested frames with gray dots. Tap the circle on each frame you want as its own photo, then tap Done. If none of the frames matter, tap the thumbnail, then the Trash button, to delete the whole burst.

Chat images: group chats that save every meme and forwarded photo to your library add thousands of files you never chose to keep. In WhatsApp, the Save to Photos setting sits under Settings > Chats; turn it off so the pile stops growing, then clear what’s already there during Step 5.

Step 5: Work year by year on what’s left

What remains is mostly photos you took on purpose. This is the pile that needs your eyes, so give it a boundary: one year per sitting.

Swipe down on the library grid and tap Years. Apple says the Years and Months views hide visual clutter like similar photos, screenshots, whiteboards and receipts, so they’re good for spotting which years are worth a closer look. To clean a year, tap All and work through it with Select and the same finger slide, picking up runs of shots you don’t want.

Leave the keepers for later. When you hit a photo you’re sure about, tap the heart to make it a Favorite and move on. Later, the library filter can show only Favorites, which gives you a short list that survived the cleanup. You don’t have to finish this step. Steps 1 to 4 do most of the space-saving; Step 5 is for tidiness.

Step 6: Check Recently Deleted before you empty it

Deleted photos stay in Recently Deleted for 30 days and keep using iPhone storage during that time, which is why your free space may not jump after a big cleanup. Apple also says they don’t count toward your iCloud storage.

Before emptying it, scroll through once at speed for anything that slipped into a row sweep. To get one back, tap Select, choose it, tap the More button, then Recover. When you’re happy, open Photos > Collections > Recently Deleted (under Utilities), tap View Album, tap Select, then More (…), then Delete All. That last step can’t be undone. Frequently asked questions

What is the fastest way to delete thousands of photos on iPhone at once?

Open a group that's safe to clear in bulk, such as Collections, then Media Types, then Screenshots. Tap Select, slide your finger across the thumbnails instead of tapping each one, then tap the Trash button. Exact copies go faster still: in the Duplicates album, tap Select, then Select All, then Merge.

Is there a way to clean up my iPhone photo library without looking at every photo?

Yes. Clear whole categories where almost nothing is worth keeping, in order of payoff: exact duplicates, screenshots, big videos, bursts and saved chat images. What's left is mostly real photos, and you can review those one year at a time later or leave them as they are.

Should I delete my old iPhone photos year by year or all at once?

Year by year, after the bulk categories are gone. One year is small enough to finish in a sitting, and the Years view hides similar photos, screenshots and receipts, so you look at the shots that matter. Tap All when you want to see every photo from that year.

Will deleting thousands of photos on my iPhone free up space right away?

Not right away. Deleted items sit in Recently Deleted for 30 days and keep using iPhone storage until they leave that album. Once you've checked it, open Recently Deleted, tap Select, then More, then Delete All.

Does deleting photos from my iPhone also delete them from iCloud and my other devices?

Yes, if iCloud Photos is on. Apple says a photo you delete on one device is deleted on every device signed in to the same Apple Account. Back up anything you want to keep somewhere else before a big cleanup.
