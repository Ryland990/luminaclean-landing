Is it Photos or System Data filling up my iPhone storage? (2026)

Is it Photos or System Data filling up my iPhone storage?

Open Settings, tap General, then tap iPhone Storage: the coloured bar and the list under it show how many gigabytes go to Photos and how many to System Data, which iOS describes as caches, logs and other resources currently in use by the system. Deleting photos only shrinks the Photos number (after Recently Deleted clears, since deleted items keep using iPhone storage for up to 30 days), while Apple says the iPhone removes cached and temporary files by itself when it needs space.

Short answer

Look at the bar in Settings > General > iPhone Storage. If Photos is the biggest segment, delete duplicates, similar shots, screenshots and large videos, then empty Recently Deleted. If System Data is the biggest, deleting photos won’t move it: restart the iPhone, give it a day or two, install the latest iOS update and offload heavy streaming or messaging apps. The two problems look the same from the outside (a “Storage Almost Full” alert) but need different fixes. Spending an evening deleting photos when System Data is the culprit frees almost nothing, and the reverse is true too. Two minutes on the storage screen tells you which one you’re dealing with.

How to read the iPhone Storage screen

Go to Settings, tap General, then tap iPhone Storage. Apple’s iPhone User Guide says the available storage is shown at the top. Under it sits a coloured bar split by category, and further down a list of apps sorted by how much space each one uses. The list can take a few seconds to fill in, so wait until the numbers stop changing before you read them.

Three lines matter for this question:

  • Photos is the photos and videos stored in the Photos app. It appears in the app list with its own size.
  • System Data sits near the bottom of the list, below the apps. Tap it and iOS describes it as caches, logs and other resources currently in use by the system. Apple’s storage support article groups the same kind of items (Siri voices, fonts, dictionaries, non-removable logs and caches, the Spotlight index) under a category it calls Other.
  • iOS is the operating system itself. Apple says this varies by device and model. You can’t shrink it, so leave it out of your sums.

Compare Photos with System Data. Whichever is bigger is where your effort should go. Also note that Apple counts Messages attachments under Messages and downloaded shows inside each streaming app, not under System Data, so check those lines in the list too.

Take a screenshot of the screen first. System Data moves up and down by itself, so a before-and-after pair is the only reliable way to tell whether anything you did made a difference.

When Photos is the big part of the bar

This is the easy case, because every photo or video you delete comes off the Photos number. The space hides in a few predictable places:

  • Large videos. A few minutes of 4K footage can outweigh thousands of photos. In Photos, tap Collections and scroll to Media Types, then Videos, to see them together.
  • Duplicates. Photos keeps an album of them under Collections, then Utilities, then Duplicates, once its background scan has found any.
  • Similar shots and bursts. Ten near-identical frames of the same moment, where you only want the best one.
  • Screenshots. Under Collections, then Media Types, then Screenshots. Old boarding passes and receipts pile up fast.

Deleting is only half the job. Apple says you can recover deleted photos and videos from Recently Deleted for 30 days, and until then they keep using iPhone storage. To get the space back now, open Photos, tap Collections, scroll to Utilities and tap Recently Deleted, then View Album, tap Select, tap More (…) and choose Delete All. That last step can’t be undone, so check the album before you clear it.

If you use iCloud Photos with Optimize iPhone Storage turned on, deleting may free less than you expect, because the iPhone already keeps smaller versions of many photos. Our guide on why Optimize iPhone Storage isn’t freeing space covers that case.

When System Data is the big part of the bar

Start by knowing what Apple says, because it rules out a lot of bad advice. Apple’s support article on checking storage says that when your device is low on storage, it frees up space by removing items you can download again or that aren’t needed, like unused apps and temporary files. It also says the iPhone removes cached and temporary files so you don’t have to. There’s no “clear System Data” button in Settings, and Apple doesn’t publish a normal size for it.

So a large System Data figure isn’t a fault by itself. It often shrinks once the phone runs low on space and starts clearing caches. The steps below are the ones people commonly report helping when it stays high. None of them is guaranteed, and Apple doesn’t list them as System Data fixes.

  1. Restart the iPhone. Commonly reported to clear some temporary files and logs. Turn it off, wait a minute, turn it back on, then check the storage screen again after a few minutes.
  2. Wait a day or two. Users commonly report System Data dropping by itself after a large download, an iOS update or a long streaming session, once the phone has had time to tidy up.
  3. Install the latest iOS update. Go to Settings, then General, then Software Update. Updates sometimes change how much the system keeps around, though Apple doesn’t promise a smaller System Data figure.
  4. Offload or delete heavy streaming, messaging and social apps. In the iPhone Storage list, tap an app and choose Offload App, which Apple says frees the storage used by the app but keeps its documents and data. Deleting the app removes the app and its related data. Reinstalling afterwards is commonly reported to bring System Data down, since some of an app’s cached data isn’t counted under the app itself. Apple notes that cached and temporary data might not count as an app’s usage.
  5. Check Messages. Apple counts messages and their attachments under Messages, not System Data, but they’re a common source of storage use. Apple’s advice is to delete conversations and attachments you no longer need.
  6. Turn on Offload Unused Apps. Go to Settings, tap Apps, tap App Store, then turn on Offload Unused Apps. Apple says the iPhone can then remove unused apps when storage is low while keeping your data.

Erasing the iPhone is a last resort

Many forum threads recommend backing up, erasing the iPhone and restoring it to shrink System Data. Some people report it works. It also takes hours, and anything that wasn’t in the backup is gone for good, so treat it as a last resort, not a first step.

If System Data is still huge after a restart, a few days of waiting, an iOS update and offloading your heaviest apps, and the phone is too full to use, make a full backup first and confirm it finished. Only then consider erasing. If you’re unsure, Apple Support can look at it with you before you wipe anything.

Frequently asked questions

Why is System Data taking up so much storage on my iPhone?

iOS describes System Data as caches, logs and other resources currently in use by the system, so it grows when apps stream, download or sync a lot. Apple says your iPhone removes cached and temporary files by itself when it needs space, which is why the number often drops without you doing anything. A figure that stays high for weeks is commonly linked to heavy streaming, messaging or social apps, but Apple doesn’t publish a list of causes.

How do I reduce System Data on iPhone?

There is no button for it. The steps people commonly report helping are restarting the iPhone, waiting a day or two, installing the latest iOS update, and offloading or deleting large streaming, messaging and social apps, then reinstalling them. None of these is guaranteed. Apple’s own advice is that the device clears cached and temporary files when it needs space.

Does deleting photos reduce System Data?

No. Photos and videos are counted under Photos in Settings > General > iPhone Storage, not under System Data. Deleting them lowers the Photos number once you also empty Recently Deleted, where deleted items keep using iPhone storage for up to 30 days.

Why does my iPhone storage say System Data is 30 GB?

Apple doesn’t publish a normal size for System Data, and users commonly report it rising and falling by several gigabytes over a few days as caches are built and cleared. If it stays around 30 GB after a restart, a few days of waiting and an iOS update, check which streaming, messaging or social apps you use most and try offloading them. Erasing the iPhone is a last resort, and only after a full backup.

Can a photo cleaner app clear System Data on iPhone?

LuminaClean can’t, and it doesn’t try. It only works on your photo library: duplicates, similar shots, screenshots and large videos, which all count under Photos. If Photos is the biggest part of your storage bar, a cleaner helps; if System Data is, it won’t change that number.
