Which photo should I keep from five nearly identical shots?

Which photo should I keep when I have five nearly identical shots?

Keep the one where the faces are sharp when you zoom in, everyone’s eyes are open and nothing is smeared by motion, then delete the other four. One keeper per moment (two at most, when they differ in expression or framing) is a good rule, and anything you delete waits in Recently Deleted for 30 days in case you change your mind.

Short answer

Open the first shot, pinch to zoom on the eyes, and check five things in order: sharp faces, open eyes, no motion blur, the best expression, a straight horizon. The first photo that passes all five is your keeper. Tap the heart to favorite it, then delete the rest: in Photos, tap Select, tap the photos you’re removing, then tap the Trash button. For a Burst, tap its thumbnail, tap Burst, pick your frames and tap Done. To find these runs, use the All view of your Library: Apple’s Duplicates album is for duplicates, so five slightly different shots of one moment usually won’t show up there.

The one-minute keeper test

Open the first photo in the run and work through these checks in order. Each one knocks out a few candidates, and by the fourth you usually have one left.

  1. Sharpness on the eyes. Pinch to zoom in on the nearest face. At thumbnail size every frame looks sharp; zoomed in, missed focus shows up as soft eyelashes and fuzzy edges. For scenery, zoom on the main subject. A sharp photo with an average expression beats a soft one with a great expression, because you can’t fix focus afterwards.
  2. Closed or half-closed eyes. In group shots this decides most runs. Check every face, not only the person in the middle.
  3. Motion blur. Look at hands, hair and anything that was moving. A smeared arm or a dog that turned its head mid-shot rules a photo out.
  4. Expression. Among the frames still standing, pick the one where people look like themselves: a real smile instead of a held one, someone mid-laugh instead of mid-word.
  5. Framing and horizon. Check that nobody’s head is cut off and the horizon is level. A crooked horizon can be fixed with the crop tool in Edit, so treat it as a tie-breaker.
Mark the winner first: tap the heart on your keeper before deleting anything, so you don’t select it by mistake.

Keep one per moment, two at most

The hard part is letting the second-best frame go. A useful rule: one photo per moment, and a second only when it shows something the first doesn’t.

  • Keep a second shot when the two differ in a way you’d notice in a year: a wide shot and a close-up, a posed smile and a candid laugh, a vertical and a horizontal shot of the same scene.
  • Delete it when the only difference is a fraction of a second or a slightly different head tilt.
  • Don’t keep spares “in case”. Four almost-identical frames next to the keeper don’t protect it; a copy in iCloud Photos or on your computer does.

Bursts: choose inside the stack

A Burst is one stack of many frames, taken when you swipe the Shutter button to the left in Camera. It shows up as a single thumbnail, so it’s easy to forget the frames inside. Apple’s steps:

  1. Open Photos and tap the Burst thumbnail.
  2. Tap Burst with the number next to it. Gray dots below the thumbnails mark the frames iOS suggests keeping.
  3. Tap the circle in the lower-right corner of each frame you want to save as an individual photo, then tap Done.

Treat the gray dots as a starting point and run the keeper test on them. To remove the whole stack, tap the Burst thumbnail, then the Delete button.

Live Photos: you may already have a better frame

If your best shot is a Live Photo, the moment you want may be a frame away from the one shown. You can choose a different frame:

  1. Open the Live Photo and tap Edit.
  2. Tap the Live Photo button.
  3. Drag the white frame to the moment you want, release, then tap Make Key Photo.
  4. Tap Done.

When everyone’s eyes are open a split second after the shot, a new key photo can rescue that picture, and the other near-identical shots can go.

How to find similar shots on your iPhone

Finding every run of near-identical shots across years of photos is the slow part, because the Photos app doesn’t have a “similar photos” view.

  • The Duplicates album. Open Photos, tap Collections, scroll to Utilities and tap Duplicates. Apple describes it as finding duplicate photos and videos, and it only appears when you have some. Tap Merge on a set and iOS keeps one photo while the rest move to Recently Deleted. Five shots taken a second apart with different poses are different photos, so expect most of your lookalike runs to be missing from it. If the album is missing, see the Duplicates album not showing.
  • Scroll the Library in All. Swipe down on the Library grid and tap All. Apple says the Years and Months views are curated to show your best shots and leave out clutter like similar photos, so the near-identical runs you’re looking for only show up in All. Look around events like birthdays and trips for rows of matching thumbnails. For the last few days, tap Collections, open a day under Recent Days and tap Show All.
  • Delete in batches. Once you’ve found a run, tap Select, tap each photo you’re removing (or slide your finger across a row to select several), then tap the Trash button and confirm.

By hand, this works for one trip. For a large library, a similar photo finder that groups the shots for you saves most of the time.

Deleting the rest without losing anything

Every photo you delete in Photos goes to the Recently Deleted album first and stays there for 30 days before it’s removed for good. To get one back, tap Collections, scroll to Utilities, tap Recently Deleted, tap View Album and confirm with Face ID or Touch ID, then select the photo, tap the More button and tap Recover.

Two things to know before a big cleanup:

  • iCloud Photos deletes everywhere. If you use iCloud Photos, a photo you delete on your iPhone is deleted on your other devices signed in to the same Apple Account too.
  • Space comes back after 30 days. Items in Recently Deleted keep using iPhone storage until they’re removed, though Apple says they don’t count towards your iCloud storage. To free the room now, open the album, tap Select, tap the More button, then Delete All. If you’re over your iCloud storage limit, Apple says deletes skip Recently Deleted and are removed immediately.

Frequently asked questions

How can I find similar photos on my iPhone and keep only the best one?

The Photos app has no similar-photos view, and Apple says the Years and Months views leave similar photos out, so swipe down on the Library grid, tap All and look for rows of matching thumbnails around events. Open each run, zoom in to compare sharpness and eyes, favorite the best shot, then tap Select, pick the others and tap the Trash button. A similar-photo cleaner such as LuminaClean groups these runs on the iPhone for you, so you only make the keep-or-delete call.

How do I pick the best photo out of a burst on my iPhone?

Tap the Burst thumbnail in Photos, then tap Burst. Gray dots under the thumbnails mark the frames iOS suggests. Zoom in to check focus and eyes, tap the circle on each frame you want to keep as its own photo, then tap Done.

Does the iPhone Duplicates album find similar photos or only duplicates?

Apple describes the Duplicates album as finding duplicate photos and videos, and doesn’t publish how it matches them. Shots taken a second apart with a different pose or expression are different photos, so most runs of lookalike shots won’t appear there. You’ll find them by scrolling your Library or with a similar-photo finder.

Should I keep more than one photo of the same moment?

Keep a second one only when it shows something the first doesn’t, such as a wide shot and a close-up, or a posed smile and a candid laugh. If the only difference is a fraction of a second, keep the sharper frame and delete the other.

Can I get a photo back if I delete the wrong one by mistake?

Yes, for 30 days. Open Photos, tap Collections, scroll to Utilities and open Recently Deleted, tap View Album, select the photo, tap the More button and tap Recover. If you’re over your iCloud storage limit, Apple says deletes skip Recently Deleted, so check that first.
