How long does iPhone take to find duplicates?

Short answer

Apple’s Duplicates album is filled by a background scan that only runs while your iPhone is locked, charging and left alone, which in practice means overnight. A small library can show duplicates within an hour or two; a library of 20,000 photos or more can take several nights, and the album doesn’t appear at all until the scan has found at least one pair. Leave the phone plugged in overnight with Low Power Mode off and iCloud Photos finished syncing, then check Albums, then Utilities, in the morning.

Apple has never published a figure for how long this takes, so every number below is what people typically see rather than something Apple promises. The scan has no progress bar, no start button and no notification when it finishes. All you get is an album that shows up once there is something to put in it.

Typical timings by library size

These ranges assume the phone is charging and locked for the whole stretch. Interrupt it and the clock stretches out.

Library sizeTypical time to see the album
Under 2,000 photosOne to two hours on the first charge
2,000 to 10,000One full night, sometimes two
10,000 to 20,000Two to three nights
20,000 and upSeveral nights, occasionally a week of overnight charges

Two things stretch those ranges further. A brand new phone restoring from a backup has to import and analyse everything at once, and an iPhone that gets unplugged every morning at 7am only ever gets a few usable hours per night.

Why it takes so long

Duplicate detection on iPhone happens entirely on the device. Nothing is sent to a server to be compared, so your phone does all the work itself, photo by photo, and that work is expensive in battery and heat terms. Apple treats it as a low-priority background job and gates it behind conditions.

  • Locked and charging. The analysis runs while the phone is on power and not in use. Wake it up and start scrolling, and the scan stops until the next idle window.
  • Low Power Mode. It suspends background tasks, so a phone left in Low Power Mode overnight makes no progress at all.
  • Nearly full storage. iOS holds back background work when free space is short. If your phone is down to its last few hundred megabytes, our guide to freeing up iPhone storage is the first step.
  • Active iCloud sync. Uploading or downloading a library takes priority. The duplicate scan waits until sync settles.
  • Heat. A warm phone throttles background processing, so charging under a pillow works against you.

The checklist to make it run tonight

You can’t make the scan go faster, but you can stop it from being paused. Do all six of these before bed:

  1. Plug the iPhone into a wall charger, not a laptop that goes to sleep.
  2. Turn Low Power Mode off in Settings, then Battery.
  3. Stay on Wi-Fi so iCloud can finish what it’s doing.
  4. Open Photos and scroll to the bottom of the Library tab. If it says it’s uploading or downloading items, wait for that to clear.
  5. Free up a gigabyte or two if storage is nearly full.
  6. Lock the screen, leave the phone somewhere cool and don’t touch it.
One habit that fixes it: charge the phone on a bedside table instead of in your hand at midnight. Two hours of evening charging while you scroll gives iOS almost no idle time to work with, and that pattern is behind a lot of albums that never show up.

How to tell whether it’s still scanning

There’s no status screen, so you have to read the album itself. Open Photos, tap Albums, scroll past your personal albums and Media Types to the Utilities section at the bottom.

If Duplicates isn’t listed, iOS hasn’t found a single pair yet. That either means the scan is still working or that your library has no exact duplicates in it at all. Both look identical from the outside, which is why the missing album confuses people.

When Duplicates is there and the count keeps climbing over the next few days, the scan is still running. iOS works through the library in passes rather than finishing in one go, so a number that goes from 12 to 60 to 340 across three mornings is normal behaviour. Once it stops moving for a couple of days, treat the scan as done.

What changed in iOS 26

The Duplicates album lives in the same place in iOS 26 as it has since iOS 16: Photos, then Albums, then Utilities. The redesigned Photos app moved a lot of things around, but not this one.

What does happen after any major iOS update is that the photo analysis gets rebuilt, so the album can go empty or vanish for a few nights while the phone re-examines the library. If you searched for this a day or two after installing iOS 26, that timing is very likely the answer. Give it the same overnight charging routine as a new phone and it comes back on its own.

When it’s been a week and there’s still nothing

Past roughly a week of proper overnight charges, waiting longer stops being the fix. At that point the cause is usually something specific: an iOS version below 16, a sync that never completes, a point-release bug, or a library with no exact duplicates in it. What the scan will never find

Worth knowing before you spend three nights waiting: Apple’s scan only matches exact and very close copies. Plenty of wasted space sits outside that definition.

  • Five shots of the same view, taken seconds apart, where one is sharp and four aren’t.
  • Burst frames from a single press of the shutter.
  • An edited crop saved next to the untouched original.
  • Photos re-saved from WhatsApp or another messaging app, which get re-encoded and become different files.

All of those look like duplicates to you and like separate photos to iOS. Frequently asked questions

Why is my Duplicates album not showing up?

The album is hidden until the background scan has found at least one duplicate pair, so a phone that hasn’t finished scanning shows nothing at all. It also sits inside Albums, then Utilities, near the bottom of the Albums tab, not in your main album list. If the scan has had several charged nights and the album still isn’t there, our guide to the Duplicates album not showing covers the other causes.

Does the iPhone duplicate scan run in the background?

Yes, and only in the background. iOS analyses your library while the iPhone is locked, connected to power and not being used, then pauses the moment you pick it up. There is no button in Photos to start the scan by hand and no progress bar while it runs.

How do I make iPhone find duplicates faster?

Plug the phone in, lock it, turn Low Power Mode off, stay on Wi-Fi, let iCloud Photos finish syncing and free up a gigabyte or two if storage is nearly full. Then leave it alone overnight. You can’t speed the scan itself up, only stop it from being paused.

Why does the Duplicates album keep finding new photos?

Because the scan works through the library in passes rather than finishing all at once. New pairs keep surfacing over several nights until iOS has compared everything, and fresh imports get analysed later as well. A count that grows for a few days is normal, not a bug.

Does iOS find similar photos too, or only exact duplicates?

Only exact and very close matches. Burst frames, edited copies saved beside the original, and photos re-encoded by messaging apps are visually similar but different files, so they never reach the Duplicates album. Catching those takes a cleaner that compares images visually instead of file by file.
