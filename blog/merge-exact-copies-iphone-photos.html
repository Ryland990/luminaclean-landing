What “Merge exact copies” does in iPhone Photos

Short answer

Tapping Merge in the Duplicates album keeps one photo, the highest-quality version, and combines the useful data from the other copies into it: captions, keywords, favorite status, album membership, and the location or date if only one copy carried them. The extra copies are not destroyed. They move to Recently Deleted, where you have 30 days to change your mind. “Merge exact copies” is the wording iOS uses when the files in a group are identical, byte for byte, and near-identical shots are labeled differently.

The Duplicates album gives you a stack of photos that look the same, a Merge button next to each pair, and a Select mode that offers to merge the lot. Nothing on the screen says which photo survives or where the others go, which is why people hesitate over the button.

Below is what merging does, where the removed copies end up, and the cases where the button is greyed out or missing.

Where the Merge button lives

The album shows up on its own once iOS has found something to put in it:

  1. Open Photos.
  2. Go to Albums, scroll to Utilities, and tap Duplicates. On iOS 18 and later the Photos app was reorganized, so Utilities sits in the collections list further down the main screen rather than behind an Albums tab.
  3. Tap Merge next to any single group to handle that group on its own.
  4. Or tap Select, pick several groups (or use Select All), then tap the merge button at the bottom.

That bottom button is where the phrasing people search for comes from. When every group you selected holds identical files, it reads something like Merge 3 Exact Copies. When the selection includes copies that aren’t identical, the wording drops “exact” and refers to duplicates instead. The exact strings have shifted between iOS releases, so treat the wording as a hint about what you selected rather than a fixed label. iOS asks you to confirm before it does anything.

What “Merge exact copies” means, and what it doesn’t

Exact copies are the same file saved more than once. Same pixels, same resolution, same encoding. This happens more than you’d think: saving an image twice from a group chat, importing the same batch from a camera roll backup, or restoring a library on top of photos that were already there.

The Duplicates album also collects copies that are close but not identical, such as the same shot saved once at full size and once smaller, or the same image in two formats. Those still count as duplicates to Apple, and the button drops the word “exact” for them. The practical difference: with identical files it makes no difference which one survives, while with near-identical ones the merge is making a quality judgment on your behalf.

What the album will not do is group photos that only look alike. Three tries at the same portrait are three separate photos to iOS, not duplicates, and they never reach this screen.

What merge keeps and what it drops

Merge keeps the highest-quality version of the group. In practice that means the largest resolution and the least compressed file. It then combines relevant data from the other copies into the keeper, so nothing useful is lost when a copy goes: captions, keywords, favorite status, album membership, and location or date information if only one copy had it.

What it drops is the redundant file itself, plus anything unique to that specific file rather than to the photo. If one copy was a lower-resolution export, those lower-resolution pixels are what leaves. You don’t get to choose the keeper in this screen. Apple picks it, which is fine for identical files and worth a glance when the group isn’t identical. Our guide on whether deleting duplicates deletes the original covers that decision in more depth.

Where the other copies go

Straight to Recently Deleted, which is the part that settles most people’s nerves. The copies removed by a merge are treated like any other deletion. They sit in Photos > Albums > Utilities > Recently Deleted for 30 days, and you can restore any of them by selecting the photo and tapping Recover. After 30 days iOS clears them for good.

So a merge is reversible in the sense that matters. You keep the merged photo and you can pull the removed copies back if you decide the wrong one survived.

What happens if iCloud Photos is on

The merge syncs. Your library is shared across every device signed into the same Apple Account, so merging on your iPhone updates your iPad and Mac too, and the removed copies land in the Recently Deleted that all your devices share. Recovery works for 30 days from any of them. It also means a merge is not a local tidy-up you can keep to one device.

Before merging a big batch: scroll the selection once and check whether the groups look identical or merely similar. Identical groups are safe to merge in bulk. Mixed groups are worth opening one at a time, because that is where Photos is picking a keeper for you.

Why Merge is greyed out or missing

Three common causes, and none of them mean something is broken:

  • Detection hasn’t finished. iOS analyzes the library while the iPhone is locked and charging. On a library of tens of thousands of photos that can take several days, and the album stays empty or partial until it’s done.
  • An iCloud sync is in progress. Photos avoids merging while items are still uploading or downloading, so the button can sit disabled until the library settles.
  • Shared items. Photos in a Shared Library or a shared album behave differently from ones you own outright, and groups involving them may not offer a merge at all.

If the album itself never appears, that’s a separate problem with its own causes. See what to do when the iPhone Duplicates album isn’t showing.

What Merge will never catch

The album is deliberately narrow. It handles exact and near-exact file copies, and stops there. Everything below stays in your library after you’ve merged every group it offered:

  • Burst frames. Ten shutter frames from one press, nine of them redundant.
  • Similar shots. Four attempts at the same photo, seconds apart, slightly different.
  • Edited versions. A cropped or filtered copy saved next to the untouched original.
  • Messaging re-saves. A photo you sent through WhatsApp and saved back is re-compressed, so it’s a different file with different pixels.

For most people that is where the bulk of the wasted space is. Merging the Duplicates album empty is a good first pass, not the whole cleanup. Our guide on deleting duplicate photos on iPhone compares the methods that go further.

Frequently asked questions

Does “Merge exact copies” delete my photos?

It removes the extra copies but keeps one, so the photo itself stays in your library. The copies it removes are moved to Recently Deleted, not erased, and you have 30 days to restore any of them. If you merge a group of three identical files, you end up with one photo in the library and two recoverable copies in Recently Deleted.

Which photo does iPhone keep when merging duplicates?

Photos keeps the highest-quality version of the group, which in practice means the largest resolution and the least compressed file. It then folds in the useful data from the other copies, such as captions, keywords, favorite status, album membership, and the location or date if only one copy carried them. You do not get to pick the keeper yourself in the Duplicates album.

Is it safe to merge all duplicates on iPhone?

For byte-identical files it is about as safe as photo cleanup gets, because every copy is the same and one of them survives. Merging a mixed batch is worth a quick scroll first, since some groups may hold copies at different resolutions or formats and Photos picks the keeper for you. Either way the removed copies sit in Recently Deleted for 30 days.

Why can’t I merge duplicates on my iPhone?

The usual reasons are that the library is still being analyzed, that an iCloud Photos sync has not finished, or that the group includes items from a Shared Library or a shared album, which Photos will not merge. Detection runs while the iPhone is locked and charging, and on a large library it can take several days before the Duplicates album is complete.

What’s the difference between exact copies and similar photos?

Exact copies are the same file saved more than once, so the pixels match byte for byte. Similar photos look alike but are different files: burst frames, three tries at the same group shot, an edited crop next to the untouched original, or a photo re-compressed by a messaging app. The Duplicates album handles exact and near-exact copies. It leaves similar shots alone, which is why most of the redundant photos in a large library never show up there.
