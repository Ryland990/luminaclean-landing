Why iPhone storage goes up after you delete photos

Why does my iPhone storage go up after I delete photos?

Deleting a photo frees no space for up to 30 days, because iOS moves it to Recently Deleted and keeps the file on your iPhone until that window ends or you empty the album. In the meantime, a big cleanup gives iOS work to do (updating the photo library, its search index and iCloud sync), and the temporary files that work creates are counted as System Data, so the used total can climb for a few hours before it drops. Empty Recently Deleted, restart, and leave the phone charging overnight. If storage is still rising the next day, iCloud is most likely downloading photos or another device or app is adding them.

Short answer

Look at which bar grew in Settings > General > iPhone Storage. Total flat, Photos unchanged: the deleted files are still in Recently Deleted. System Data up: temporary caches and logs from the cleanup, which iOS clears on its own. Photos up: iCloud is pulling full-size copies down, or new photos are arriving from another device, a Shared Library or a chat app. Only the last one keeps growing for days.

Apple doesn’t explain this exact symptom anywhere, so below we separate what Apple documents from what people commonly report.

Cause 1: Recently Deleted still holds every file

Apple’s own wording is plain: when you delete photos and videos, they go to your Recently Deleted album for 30 days. You can restore anything from there, which means the full file is still on your phone. Deleting 2 GB of videos moves 2 GB between albums and frees nothing.

That’s why the total stays flat. It also explains a confusing split: Apple says items in Recently Deleted don’t count towards your iCloud storage, so your iCloud number can drop right away while your iPhone number doesn’t move.

How to tell: open Recently Deleted. If it holds hundreds of items, that’s your missing space.

Fix: in Photos, scroll down to Utilities, tap Recently Deleted and open it with Face ID. Tap Select, then the More button (…), then Delete All, and confirm. This step is permanent, so scan the album first. Our guide on whether Recently Deleted photos take up storage covers the iCloud prompts you may see.

Cause 2: System Data grows while iOS processes the cleanup

This is the one that makes the number go up. Apple describes the System Data bucket as logs and caches, the Spotlight index, and system data such as the Keychain and CloudKit database. A large deletion touches several of those at once: the library database changes, the search index is rebuilt, and with iCloud Photos on, every deletion is queued for sync.

Apple doesn’t say how much space that work uses. What’s commonly reported is a near one-for-one swap. In one Apple Community thread, a user deleted 527 items, watched Photos shrink by 1.8 GB, and saw the other category grow by 1.83 GB. In our experience that growth is usually temporary.

Two Apple details explain why it lingers. Apple says photo analysis requires iPhone to be locked and connected to power, so a phone that never sits on a charger never finishes. And Apple says the device removes cached and temporary files when it needs more space, which means iOS isn’t in a hurry to clean up while you still have room.

How to tell: Photos went down, System Data went up by a similar amount.

Fix: restart the iPhone, then leave it locked on a charger and on Wi-Fi overnight. Check again the next day. See how long iPhone storage takes to update after deleting photos for the full timeline.

Cause 3: The Settings screen hasn’t finished counting

The iPhone Storage screen rebuilds its numbers each time you open it, and the categories fill in over several seconds. Read it while it’s still calculating and you can catch Photos already updated but System Data not yet trimmed, which looks like growth. This is an observed pattern rather than something Apple documents.

How to tell: the numbers change between two visits a few minutes apart, with nothing deleted in between.

Fix: close Settings from the app switcher, reopen it, and wait for the bar to settle. Still wrong? Restart and check again.

Cause 4: iCloud Photos is downloading

With Optimize iPhone Storage on, which is the default, Apple says full-resolution photos and videos are stored in iCloud, with storage-saving versions kept on your iPhone. Reviewing photos before deleting them means opening and zooming into them, and the phone fetches larger versions to show you. In our experience those copies stick around for a while, so a long review session can add space before the deletions free any.

The bigger version of this happens if you switched to Download and Keep Originals, sometimes on advice to “fix” storage. That setting puts every original back on the phone, and Apple says syncing a large library may need to run overnight. Photos storage climbs the whole time.

How to tell: open Photos and tap your profile picture. The library status under your name and photo count shows whether it’s uploading or downloading. Then check Settings, tap your name, iCloud, Photos, and see which storage option is selected.

Fix: if you’re short on space, choose Optimize iPhone Storage and let the sync finish on Wi-Fi.

Cause 5: Something is still adding photos

Your camera isn’t the only source. Apple notes that with iCloud Photos, changes you make on one device are reflected on your other devices, so an iPad or Mac importing photos fills your phone too. An iCloud Shared Photo Library lets up to five other people add photos and videos. WhatsApp can save every received photo to your camera roll, and Messages keeps its own attachments outside Photos.

Bugs are rare. In May 2024, Apple shipped iOS 17.5.1 to fix a rare issue where photos that experienced database corruption could reappear after being deleted. If old photos you deleted are back in your library, update iOS before anything else.

How to tell: sort your library by Recently Added. New items you didn’t take point to another device, a shared library or a chat app.

Fix: turn off the chat app’s save-to-photos setting, check the Shared Library, and look at Messages in Settings > General > iPhone Storage.

How to check which one it is

Work through these in order. Steps 1 to 5 take about ten minutes.

  1. Note the used total and the Photos and System Data figures in Settings > General > iPhone Storage.
  2. Open Recently Deleted. If it’s full, empty it.
  3. Check the library status in Photos for uploading or downloading.
  4. Check whether iCloud Photos is set to Optimize or to Download and Keep Originals.
  5. Sort by Recently Added and look for photos you didn’t take.
  6. Restart, charge overnight on Wi-Fi, then compare with the numbers from step 1.

If all six come back clean and the phone is still full, the space is somewhere else. Our guide to why iPhone storage is still full after deleting photos covers Messages, app caches and the other usual spots.

What not to do first: don’t factory reset. In an Apple Developer Forums thread on growing System Data, users report a reset gave only temporary relief, and one says turning iCloud off and on again pushed it from 25 GB to 40 GB. Don’t pay for an app that promises to clear System Data either. iOS doesn’t give other apps access to it.

Where the space usually is

Once Recently Deleted is empty and System Data has settled, the photos still taking up room are the ones nobody deleted. In our experience, that’s the same four groups on almost every phone: exact duplicates, bursts of near-identical shots where one is sharp and the rest aren’t, screenshots that did their job months ago, and a handful of long videos. Frequently asked questions

Why is my iPhone storage increasing after deleting photos?

Deleted photos stay on your iPhone in Recently Deleted for 30 days, so deleting them frees nothing at first. Meanwhile, iOS writes temporary caches, logs and index updates while it processes a big cleanup, and those count as System Data. Empty Recently Deleted, restart, and charge overnight; the total usually drops once iOS clears that temporary data.

Why does my Photos storage go up when I delete photos?

Photos still counts everything in Recently Deleted, so deleting alone won’t shrink it. If the Photos figure grows, iCloud Photos is usually downloading larger versions, often after a switch to Download and Keep Originals, or new photos are arriving from another device, a Shared Library or a chat app. The library status under your name in Photos shows whether a download is running.

Why does my iPhone storage keep filling up after deleting?

If it keeps rising for days, something is adding data faster than you remove it. Common sources are iCloud downloading originals, WhatsApp saving received media to your camera roll, Messages attachments, and photos synced from an iPad or Mac. Settings > General > iPhone Storage shows which category is growing.

How long does it take for iPhone storage to go down after deleting photos?

Up to 30 days if you leave the photos in Recently Deleted, because each item is removed 30 days after you delete it. Empty the album yourself and the space comes back within minutes. Any System Data bump from the cleanup usually settles after a restart and a night on the charger.

Can I clear System Data on my iPhone myself?

Not directly. There’s no button for it in Settings, and Apple says the device removes cached and temporary files on its own when it needs the space. A restart and an overnight charge on Wi-Fi give iOS the chance to tidy up, and no third-party app can do it for you.
