How long until iPhone storage updates after deleting photos?

Short answer

Deleting a photo frees nothing immediately, because it sits in Recently Deleted for up to 30 days. Once you empty Recently Deleted, the space is released within seconds to a few minutes. The number in Settings > General > iPhone Storage can lag behind what actually changed, and it refreshes when you reopen that screen or restart the phone. With iCloud Photos on, the local change can take longer, since your iPhone re-syncs with iCloud first.

You deleted a few hundred photos, went back to Settings, and the storage bar looks exactly the same. That’s normal, and it isn’t a bug. iOS treats deletion as two separate events: the moment you remove a photo from your library, and the moment the file actually leaves your device. Those can be a month apart.

Here’s the timeline, what each stage costs you in time, and how to push the storage number to update instead of waiting on it.

The timeline, stage by stage

Stage Space freed How long it takes
You delete photos in the Photos app None They sit in Recently Deleted for up to 30 days
You empty Recently Deleted yourself The full amount Seconds to a few minutes
The Settings storage readout — Can lag; reopen iPhone Storage or restart
iCloud Photos is on Often less than expected Minutes to hours, depending on sync

The first row is where nearly everyone gets stuck. People ask how long the iPhone Recently Deleted album keeps things, expecting a few hours. It’s 30 days per item, counted from the day you deleted that photo, so the album drains in waves instead of clearing all at once. Every one of those files is still taking up space on your phone in the meantime. If you want the storage back today, you have to empty the album by hand.

How to empty Recently Deleted

  1. Open Photos.
  2. Go to Albums, scroll to Utilities, and tap Recently Deleted. On iOS 18 and later the Photos layout changed, so look for Utilities further down the single scrolling view.
  3. Unlock the album with Face ID, Touch ID, or your passcode. iOS locks it by default from iOS 16 onward.
  4. Tap Select, then Delete All. To clear only part of it, pick individual items and tap Delete.
  5. Confirm. The storage comes back within seconds for a few hundred photos, or a couple of minutes for a very large batch of videos.

This step is permanent. Anything you clear out here is no longer recoverable through Photos, so scan the album before you tap Delete All. If you already emptied it and want something back, our guide on recovering permanently deleted photos on iPhone covers the remaining options.

How to force the storage readout to refresh

The bar in Settings is a cached snapshot, not a live meter. It often shows the numbers from before your cleanup. Three things push it to recount, in order of effort:

  • Leave and come back. Swipe Settings closed from the app switcher, reopen it, and go to General, then iPhone Storage.
  • Wait for “Calculating…”. The bar rebuilds itself for a few seconds when the screen opens. Read the numbers after that finishes, not while the categories are still filling in.
  • Restart the iPhone. A restart forces a full recount and flushes temporary files that inflate the totals. Give it a minute after it boots before checking.

If the number is still wrong an hour after a restart, the readout isn’t the problem. Something else is holding the space.

Why iCloud Photos makes it slower

With iCloud Photos on, a deletion is a sync operation, not a local one. Your iPhone marks the photo as deleted, tells iCloud, and the change propagates to your iPad and Mac. That shared Recently Deleted album still runs on the 30-day clock, so emptying it on one device removes the files everywhere. Depending on your connection and how much is queued, the sync itself can take minutes or hours to settle.

There’s a second effect that catches people out. If Optimize iPhone Storage is on, most of your full-resolution originals live in iCloud and your phone only keeps smaller versions locally. Deleting 5 GB worth of photos in that setup frees far less than 5 GB on the device, because the device never held 5 GB of them. The iCloud figure drops by the full amount; the local one doesn’t. Check which mode you’re in under Settings > Photos. For more on how deletions travel between devices, see our post on a cleaner app deleting photos from iCloud.

Quick check: if the local number barely moved but iCloud storage dropped as expected, Optimize iPhone Storage is on and it’s working as designed. Videos are where the real local savings are in that setup.

What it means when System Data grows

A common sight after a big cleanup: photos shrink, System Data jumps, and the total barely changes. System Data is the bucket iOS uses for caches, logs, indexes, and temporary files, and a large deletion generates plenty of all four while the library reindexes.

Your photos aren’t hiding in there. The category is temporary, and it usually settles over the following day or two as iOS clears what it no longer needs. Restarting the phone speeds that up. Nothing in Settings lets you clear System Data directly, and any app claiming to do it on iOS is overpromising.

When it’s not a wait, it’s a different problem

The waiting explanation covers most cases. It stops being the answer once all of these are true: Recently Deleted is empty, you’ve restarted, iCloud sync has finished, and a day has passed. At that point the space is being held by something other than the photos you removed.

Usual suspects are Messages attachments, app caches from WhatsApp, TikTok, Instagram or YouTube, downloaded video in streaming apps, and podcast episodes. Frequently asked questions

How long do photos stay in Recently Deleted?

Up to 30 days. iOS clears each item 30 days after you deleted it, so the album empties in waves rather than all at once. The countdown is per photo, and each thumbnail shows the days it has left. Until an item leaves that album, it still occupies storage on your iPhone.

Why hasn’t my storage gone down after deleting photos?

Almost always because the photos are still in Recently Deleted, which holds them for 30 days and keeps the files on your device the whole time. Empty that album and the space is released within seconds to a few minutes. If you already emptied it, the Settings readout is probably stale, so reopen iPhone Storage or restart the phone.

Does deleting photos free up iCloud storage immediately?

No. The same 30-day rule applies in iCloud, and the deletion has to sync before anything changes. Once you empty Recently Deleted on one device, the removal syncs to your other devices and the iCloud figure updates, though Apple can take a while to recalculate the number shown in Settings.

How do I refresh iPhone storage after deleting?

Close Settings completely, reopen it, and go to General, then iPhone Storage. Wait for the Calculating state at the top of the bar to finish instead of reading the old numbers. If it still looks wrong, restart the iPhone and check again, since a restart forces iOS to recount and to flush temporary files.

Why does System Data go up after I delete photos?

System Data is a catch-all for caches, logs, and temporary files, and a large deletion creates work that lands there for a while. It is not your photos hiding in another category. It usually shrinks on its own over the next day or two, and a restart speeds that up.
