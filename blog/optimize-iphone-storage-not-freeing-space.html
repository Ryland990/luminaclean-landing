Why isn’t Optimize iPhone Storage freeing up space?

Why isn’t Optimize iPhone Storage freeing up space?

Optimize iPhone Storage doesn’t shrink your library on a schedule: Apple says iCloud Photos swaps full-size files for smaller versions only when your iPhone needs more storage, starting with the photos and videos you open least. It also can’t shrink anything that hasn’t uploaded yet, so a paused sync (Low Power Mode, a battery under 20 percent, or a full iCloud plan) leaves full-size copies on the phone.

Short answer

Check three screens in this order. Photos > Collections: the icon in the top-right corner shows whether uploads are paused or your iCloud storage is full. Settings > [your name] > iCloud > Photos: confirm Optimize iPhone Storage is selected, not Download and Keep Originals (in iOS 27 it’s one level down, under iPhone Storage). Settings > General > iPhone Storage: see whether the space is in Photos at all. If uploads are done and you still have plenty of free space, nothing is broken. iOS hasn’t needed the room yet.

How Optimize iPhone Storage works

With Optimize iPhone Storage on, Apple says full-resolution photos and videos are stored in iCloud in their original formats, with storage-saving versions kept on your iPhone. It’s on by default.

The timing is the part most people miss. Apple’s iCloud Photos article says when you need more device storage, iCloud Photos optimizes your library, starting with photos and videos you access least. It isn’t a one-time cleanup that runs when you tap the setting, and there’s no button to force it.

Two conditions follow from that. The original has to be safely in iCloud before the phone can keep only a smaller version, so anything still waiting to upload stays full size. And the phone has to be short on space before it bothers. Cause 1: Your iPhone isn’t low on space yet

Apple’s storage article describes the same behaviour for the whole phone: when your device is low on storage, it frees up space by removing items that you can download again or aren’t needed. With 30 GB free, iOS has no reason to shrink your photos, so the Photos figure can sit high for months.

How to tell: open Settings > General > iPhone Storage and look at the free space at the top. Plenty free, uploads finished, Optimize selected: that’s the setting working as designed.

Fix: nothing to fix. iOS will make room when something needs it. If you need the space today rather than when iOS decides, you’ll have to remove files yourself.

Cause 2: The upload hasn’t finished or is paused

Apple lists the reasons iCloud Photos pauses in its guide to iCloud Photos not syncing:

  • Low Power Mode or Low Data Mode. Syncing pauses to save battery or data.
  • Low Battery. Syncing pauses when the charge is below 20 percent.
  • Optimizing Battery Power, Optimizing System Performance or Poor Network Connection. Tap Sync Now or wait a few minutes.
  • Needs to Cool Down. The phone is too warm to keep going.

A large library can take a long time. Apple suggests connecting to power and Wi-Fi and leaving the device to sync overnight.

How to tell: in iOS 26 and later, open Photos and tap Collections. Apple says the top-right corner previews your sync status, and a partially filled gray outline around your account icon means uploads are paused, while a partially filled blue circle means photos are still uploading. Tap the profile button and the details appear below your name and photo count.

Fix: plug in, connect to Wi-Fi, and turn off Low Power Mode (Settings > Battery > Power Mode on iPhone 15 and later, or Settings > Battery on iPhone 14 and earlier). Then leave the phone locked overnight. In iOS 27 you can also tap Sync Immediately, then For Today, on the same profile screen; Apple notes it pauses in Low Power Mode or Low Data Mode too. Check for an album named Unable to Upload, and don’t turn iCloud Photos off and on again. Apple says that restarts the sync process.

Cause 3: Your iCloud storage is full

This one stops Optimize completely. Apple says that if you run out of iCloud storage, new photos and videos won’t upload to iCloud Photos. Nothing new reaches iCloud, so nothing new can be swapped for a smaller version on the phone, and every photo you take stays full size.

How to tell: in Photos > Collections, a red exclamation mark in the top-right corner means your iCloud storage is full, and a yellow warning icon means it’s almost full. You can see the exact figure in Settings > [your name] > iCloud.

Fix: upgrade to a bigger iCloud+ plan, or make room in iCloud. One thing to know before you start deleting: Apple says that over the limit, your device immediately removes any photos and videos you delete, so they skip Recently Deleted and can’t be recovered.

Cause 4: Download and Keep Originals is selected

Optimize is the default, but it’s easy to switch off, and some “fix your photos” advice tells people to. With Download and Keep Originals selected, every full-size file stays on the phone no matter how full it gets.

How to tell and fix: in iOS 26, go to Settings > [your name] > iCloud > Photos and select Optimize iPhone Storage. In iOS 27, Apple moved the choice one level down: Settings > [your name] > iCloud > Photos > iPhone Storage.

Cause 5: Recently Deleted is still holding files

Deleted photos stay in Recently Deleted for 30 days and keep using iPhone storage the whole time. Apple says items in Recently Deleted don’t count towards your iCloud storage, so your iCloud bar can drop while the phone bar doesn’t move.

Fix: open Photos > Collections > Recently Deleted (under Utilities), tap View Album, then Select, then More (…), then Delete All. That step is permanent, so look through the album first. Our guide on whether Recently Deleted photos take up storage has the details.

Cause 6: The space isn’t in Photos

Optimize only touches your photo library. If the big bar in Settings > General > iPhone Storage belongs to Messages, an app or System Data, no photo setting will move it. Apple describes that last category as non-removable logs and caches, the Spotlight index, and system data such as Keychain and CloudKit database, which iOS manages itself.

Fix: tap the largest apps in the list to see what they hold, and clear Messages attachments you don’t need. Our guide to why iPhone storage is still full after deleting photos covers the other usual spots.

If your iCloud plan is full and you don’t want to pay for more

Once uploads are done and settings are right, the remaining lever is a smaller library. That’s what frees iCloud space, and it’s also why you should only delete what you don’t want anywhere.

Frequently asked questions

Does Optimize iPhone Storage delete photos from my iPhone?

No. It keeps a smaller, storage-saving version of each photo and video on your iPhone and stores the full-resolution original in iCloud. Your library looks the same, and Apple says you can download the originals over Wi-Fi or cellular.

How long does Optimize iPhone Storage take to free up space?

There’s no set time. Apple says iCloud Photos optimizes your library when your device needs more storage, so space comes back as the phone runs short, not on a schedule. Uploads have to finish first, and Apple suggests leaving a large library on power and Wi-Fi overnight.

Can I force Optimize iPhone Storage to free up space right now?

No, there’s no button for it. You can make sure uploads aren’t paused, turn off Low Power Mode and, in iOS 27, use Sync Immediately so new items upload first. If you need space today, you have to remove files yourself.

Why are my photos still taking up so much space with iCloud Photos on?

Usually one of four things: your iPhone isn’t low on storage yet, uploads are paused or your iCloud plan is full, Download and Keep Originals is selected, or Recently Deleted still holds files for up to 30 days. Optimized versions take some space too, so Photos never drops to zero.

Does deleting photos on my iPhone free up iCloud storage too?

Yes, with iCloud Photos on. Apple says deleting a photo on one device deletes it on all your devices, and items in Recently Deleted don’t count towards iCloud storage. The iPhone keeps using space for those items until Recently Deleted is emptied or 30 days pass.
