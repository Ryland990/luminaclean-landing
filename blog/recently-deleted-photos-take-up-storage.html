Do photos in Recently Deleted take up storage?

Short answer

Yes. Photos and videos in Recently Deleted still count against your iPhone storage, and against your iCloud storage if iCloud Photos is on, for up to 30 days or until you empty the album. Deleting a photo only moves it; emptying Recently Deleted is the step that gives the space back.

You spent twenty minutes deleting photos, opened Settings to admire the result, and the free-space number looks the same as before. Nothing went wrong. The files are still on the phone, sitting in an album called Recently Deleted, waiting out a countdown.

Does Recently Deleted take up storage? Yes, all of it

Deleting a photo on an iPhone doesn’t erase anything. iOS moves the file into Recently Deleted and marks it for removal in 30 days. The file keeps its full size on the same drive, so a 4 GB delete session frees 0 GB until that album is cleared.

That covers everything in there: photos, videos, Live Photos, screenshots, screen recordings. Tap into the album and each item shows how many days it has left. If iCloud Photos is on, those items keep using your iCloud storage for the same window, which is why an iPhone cleanup often fails to stop the “iCloud storage is full” notifications.

Why Apple keeps them for 30 days

It’s an undo buffer. People delete the wrong photo constantly, especially when they’re moving fast through a big library, and a delete that took effect instantly would be unforgiving. The 30-day window keeps any mistake two taps from being reversed.

Safety costs space, and Apple left the second step to you. If you do nothing, iOS clears each item on its own once its 30 days are up. Waiting a month is rarely the plan when the phone is refusing to record video today.

How to empty Recently Deleted on iPhone

This is the step that frees the space:

  1. Open the Photos app.
  2. Go to Albums. On iOS 18 and later, scroll down the main Photos screen to the collections instead.
  3. Scroll to Utilities and tap Recently Deleted.
  4. Authenticate with Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode. Since iOS 16 the album is locked by default.
  5. Tap Select in the top right.
  6. Tap Delete All in the bottom left, then confirm.

To clear only part of it, tap the items you want gone and use Delete rather than Delete All. Anything you tap Recover on goes back to your library at its original date, not to the end of your camera roll.

Look before you empty. This is the last screen where a deleted photo can be brought back from the phone alone. Scroll through once, recover anything you regret, and only then hit Delete All.

What happens with iCloud Photos on

With iCloud Photos on, your library and its Recently Deleted album are shared across every device signed into your Apple account. Emptying the album on one device empties it everywhere, and your iCloud storage total drops to match. That’s the behaviour you want if the goal is to stop paying for a bigger iCloud plan.

One nuance: if Optimize iPhone Storage is on, some of what you deleted was a smaller local version rather than a full-resolution file, so iCloud gets the bigger win and the on-device number moves less than you expected. Why the storage number lags after emptying

Emptying the album is close to instant, but the figure under Settings, General, iPhone Storage often isn’t. iOS rebuilds that breakdown in the background and can take several minutes to catch up, sometimes longer on a big library. Some of the freed space also sits in System Data for a while before it settles.

Two things help: leave the phone alone for ten minutes and check again, or restart it, which forces a recalculation.

Videos are where the space is

If you want the number to move, look at what you deleted rather than how much. A minute of 4K video at 60 fps runs to roughly 400 MB, so ten deleted clips can outweigh a thousand deleted photos. Screen recordings and slow-motion clips sit in the same bracket. Recently Deleted has no sort-by-size option, but videos carry a duration badge and are easy to pick out, and clearing those first is the fastest way to get gigabytes back. Deleting a photo is not the same as hiding it

People mix these two up. Hiding moves a photo into the Hidden album, also under Utilities, and takes it out of your main grid and Memories. It never touches storage. A hidden 200 MB video costs exactly 200 MB, forever. Hiding is for privacy; deleting, then emptying Recently Deleted, is for space. If your library grew and you can’t account for it, the Hidden album is worth a look.

Still full after emptying Recently Deleted?

Once the album is empty and the number has settled, whatever is left isn’t photos. The usual suspects are Messages attachments, which keep every video anyone ever sent you unless you set messages to auto-delete; app caches from WhatsApp, TikTok, Instagram and YouTube, which can each hold several gigabytes; and the System Data category, which absorbs logs, caches and update files. Frequently asked questions

Do photos in Recently Deleted take up iCloud storage?

Yes. If iCloud Photos is on, a deleted photo moves into a Recently Deleted album that syncs across your devices, and it keeps using iCloud storage for the full 30 days. Your iCloud total only drops once the album is emptied or the 30 days run out.

How do I permanently delete photos from Recently Deleted?

Open Photos, go to Albums, scroll to Utilities, and tap Recently Deleted. Since iOS 16 the album is locked, so authenticate with Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode. Tap Select in the top right, then Delete All in the bottom left, and confirm. To remove only some items, tap them first and then tap Delete.

Why is my storage still full after emptying Recently Deleted?

Give it a few minutes first, because iOS recalculates the storage breakdown in the background and a restart speeds it up. If the number is still high after that, photos were not the main problem. Messages attachments, app caches and System Data are the usual causes.

Can I recover photos after emptying Recently Deleted?

Not from the phone itself. Emptying the album removes the files, and iOS has no second recycle bin behind it. The only route back is a backup made before the deletion, such as an iCloud backup, a computer backup, or a copy in another service like Google Photos.

Do deleted videos take up storage in Recently Deleted?

Yes, and they are usually the largest part of it. A minute of 4K video runs to several hundred megabytes, so a handful of deleted clips can hold more space than hundreds of deleted photos. Sorting by size is not available in Recently Deleted, but the videos are easy to spot by their duration badge.
